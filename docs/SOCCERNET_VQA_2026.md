# SoccerNet VQA 2026 in the Jev decision dataset

The source is pinned to [`SoccerNet/SN-VQA-2026`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SoccerNet/SN-VQA-2026) revision `e8312fd21ece3c11ad42a6639cca3149669c8fce`. Its four answer options remain in source order, with the source `closeA` label. We do not synthesize easy distractors or relabel the answer. The full metadata manifest lives on the Modal `vl-jev-general-v1-data` volume at `/general-data/soccer_vqa_2026`; media stays on `vl-jev-general-v1-media` or at the source. Nothing is downloaded to the workstation.

The source has 8,000 train, 2,000 validation, and 500 public test questions. After checking for zero-byte media, the indexed usable rows are 7,811, 1,950, and 496. The 243 excluded rows are recorded in `summary.json` with exact source indices and reasons. Most failures are empty frame folders or answer keys absent from the supplied options. Jersey Number Recognition has no usable training or validation images in the source archive after this check, so it needs another verified source before training. Four public test rows have an answer key absent from their options, so any reported score over 496 rows is **not** the official 500-question score. Do not compare it as if it were a challenge leaderboard entry. The 2026 challenge set has withheld answers and is never training data.

All 14 source task types are audited, including text background knowledge, image background knowledge, replay grounding, and multi-view foul questions; 13 have usable training examples. Text knowledge is reported separately from visually grounded questions. Frame-folder references are expanded to numeric frame order; at most 16 evenly spaced nonempty images are selected per folder. For mixed media or multiple videos, the frozen encoder receives at most 16 frames total across the supplied assets.

## Current pilot

The balanced pilot materializes up to 32 training and eight validation questions per usable labeled task type: 416 train and 104 validation rows, plus all 496 usable public test rows. This is a pilot, not the full 10,257-row usable corpus. The complete manifests preserve the remaining candidates for later materialization. `modal_build_soccer_vqa.py --mode all --split train|valid|test` materializes every usable row of a split. Training further excludes exact source-media overlap with the public test or validation splits; validation excludes overlap with the public test.

The post-training script continues the published `general-head-v2` checkpoint (`e7771cb40f7cc41b5847e2a4880fb498572c86128c5d20cd0cce4f880211f780`) with the same frozen Jev-Omni backbone, which is built on **Gemma 4 12B IT**. It replays sampled v2 features while training the decision head, and selects a checkpoint only if it improves SoccerNet validation accuracy while retaining v2 development accuracy within the specified guard. Baseline and continued heads are both scored on the untouched public test once the feature extraction and training stages finish. This is a closed-choice accuracy study; raw score concentration is not calibrated probability.

The first pilot selected 411 overlap-filtered training rows and 104 validation rows. Validation accuracy moved from 48.1% to 51.9%. On the 496 usable public test rows, the published v2 head answered 242 correctly (48.8%) and the continued head answered 259 (52.2%). The paired change was 29 newly correct answers and 12 newly incorrect answers, with a 95% paired bootstrap interval of 1.0 to 5.8 percentage points for the accuracy difference. The continued checkpoint SHA-256 is `84348b8a0711b29780826625c04d47b22c41794bf00b0e5a7d08d650c77f835c`; its weights and the evaluation report remain on the Modal training volume under `/general-runs/soccer-vqa-posttrain-v2/`. The v2 development retention guard passed. This is evidence of transfer, but the absolute result remains far below the published leading system.

Test accuracy by modality after continuation was 45.4% on 229 video questions, 63.6% on 88 single-image questions, 70.2% on 47 image-sequence questions, and 50.0% on 132 text-only questions. Video and text need the most work. The encoder currently sees silent sampled frames, so audio/commentary evidence and match-level retrieval are plausible next additions to test before committing to a full backbone retrain.

The first pilot's overall validation gain **did not transfer to the directly visual categories**: the published head answered 37/64 and the continued head 35/64. A separate visual-only pilot trained on 256 examples and reached 36/64 at its best epoch, so the retention/accuracy guard kept the original head. These small category slices are diagnostic, not separate held-out benchmarks. The full-data visual comparison trains only on eight source categories with direct image/video evidence: action classification, camera status classification/switching, game state, jersey color, multi-view foul, replay grounding, and score/time. Background-knowledge text and image QA, match-situation QA, and commentary categories are excluded from that focused training run pending evidence audits. The source test split omits task labels, so its broad `vision` subtotal is **not** a verified visually answerable subset and must never be presented as one. The principal fast-decision benchmark needs a manually reviewed, match-disjoint set where each answer is inferable from exactly the input supplied to the model; report end-to-end latency alongside accuracy.

Before full-data continuation, the published head answered **415/847 (49.0%)** of the overlap-filtered validation rows in those eight directly visual categories. Action classification was 73/200 (36.5%) and replay grounding 23/80 (28.8%); score/time was 82/118 (69.5%) and multi-view foul 34/50 (68.0%). The all-category validation baseline was 903/1,946 (46.4%). These scores are on the existing feature policy with at most 16 sampled frames, so low temporal scores do not by themselves establish that the Gemma backbone cannot perceive the event when shown a better frame window.

## Full-data comparisons

The all-category continuation used 7,750 overlap-filtered train rows and 1,946 validation rows. Its selected epoch 3 checkpoint is `e91aa4dd2c545c7834040fe6bff42b5c6b1e18604bbe70007c06a5fb1b12101e`. All-category validation rose from 903/1,946 (46.4%) to 1,084/1,946 (55.7%), and the 496-row public test from 242/496 (48.8%) to 296/496 (59.7%). The v2 development guard passed at 6,810/7,813 (87.16%) versus 6,812/7,813 (87.19%) before. **Directly visual validation remained flat**, 415/847 before versus 414/847 after. The public test's image/video subtotal rose from 178/364 (48.9%) to 208/364 (57.1%), but lacks source task labels and may include knowledge questions.

The visual-only continuation used 3,358 train rows and 847 validation rows. Its selected epoch 3 checkpoint is `afc25a402755ba0dd6ef0b49fffd26acf13cff65af3e1b2ed89b82cd88929a56`. The labeled visual validation moved from 415/847 (49.0%) to 420/847 (49.6%). The broad public-test image/video subtotal was 182/364 (50.0%), with overall public test at 221/496 (44.6%). The v2 development guard passed at 6,822/7,813 (87.32%). Because the same validation rows selected the epoch, this five-question visual gain is an optimistic diagnostic, not a held-out improvement claim. The result does not support further head-only training as the main path to high visual accuracy.

For the complete usable corpus, `modal_build_soccer_vqa.py --mode parallel-all` distributes media checks across eight training and four validation workers, then writes verified `all-32-train.jsonl` and `all-8-valid.jsonl` manifests on the Modal data volume. `modal_soccer_vqa_posttrain.py --mode full-pipeline` waits for those manifests, extracts frozen features in resumable 64-row packs, continues the v2 head with old-data replay, and scores the same 496-row public test. Both coordinator jobs run on Modal; their result and checkpoint stay in the training volume under `/general-runs/soccer-vqa-posttrain-full-v1/`.

`modal_soccer_vqa_posttrain.py --mode direct-visual-pipeline` waits for those same cached features, then trains and scores the eight-category visual subset in its own run at `/general-runs/soccer-vqa-posttrain-full-v1-direct-visual/`. It does not alter the all-category run. Report its category-labeled validation subtotal as the direct-visual diagnostic; the public test retains only broad modality labels.

## Training choices

| Choice | Starting weights | Trainable parameters | Useful comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Continued head (implemented pilot) | Published v2 decision head | 256-slot head | Fastest estimate of transfer to subtle soccer decisions and retention on prior tasks. |
| Fresh head on frozen backbone | Newly initialized 256-slot head | 256-slot head | Measures how much the v2 head initialization helps; requires the same cached features and an isolated run. |
| Adapter fine-tuning | Published v2 head and Jev-Omni backbone | Vision/language adapters plus head | Tests whether perception needs updating; requires an end-to-end training path and larger GPU allocation. |
| Full backbone retraining | Published Jev-Omni weights or a specified base checkpoint | All selected backbone and head weights | Highest compute and memory cost; requires source-rights review, an end-to-end training path, and a fresh model artifact. |

For a valid comparison, hold the 496 usable public test rows and frame policy fixed; choose hyperparameters only on validation; report the original v2 task scores alongside soccer accuracy and accuracy by task type. Preserve an untouched subset of validation for calibration before treating scores as probabilities. For the complete corpus, resolve source match overlap across splits before training and audit duplicate questions and media. The source card lists CC BY-SA 4.0, but broadcast media rights should be checked before redistribution or commercial use.

The [SoccerNet 2026 results paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.07320) reports 97.6% for the winning system on the 500-question public test and 98.0% on the hidden challenge split. The systems use task routing, retrieval, and specialized visual tools in addition to a VLM, so a small continued decision head is an informative baseline rather than an equivalent architecture. The [challenge rules](https://github.com/jyrao/SoccerAgent/blob/main/Challenge.md) set April 24, 2026 as the submission deadline. Our 496-row test result must be reported separately from those 500-row scores until the four malformed rows have an official resolution.

The model's intended target is a fast closed-choice decision given sufficient supplied evidence. Compare it with direct Gemma 4 12B IT on **the same** curated visual questions and exact media policy before deciding whether the Jev head or the backbone is the main bottleneck. Gemma 4 12B Unified processes visual patches inside the same transformer rather than through a separate vision tower, per the [Google model card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-12B-it). The [Jev-Omni model card](https://huggingface.co/akhilaaa3/Jev-Omni) reports warm H200 medians of 26 ms for an image and 504 ms for a 16-frame video, excluding preprocessing and network time; any sub-100 ms claim for raw video requires a measured optimized or precomputed-feature path. A first adapter study should update limited Gemma transformer/multimodal-projection parameters and the decision head, retain the v2 development guard, and report both accuracy and end-to-end latency. Full backbone tuning requires stronger evidence and more reviewed visual training examples.

Because the current feature extraction embeds media together with each question and its options, the same match clip is encoded again for a different question. If the serving target is multiple millisecond-scale decisions on one scene, test a separate reusable scene embedding plus a small question/option scorer and measure the full input-to-answer path. A cached-head timing alone would omit the expensive visual step.
